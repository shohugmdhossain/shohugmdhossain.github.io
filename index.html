Overview

Advancing AI-driven discovery across Earth and planetary landscapes.

This website highlights research at the intersection of geospatial artificial intelligence, remote sensing, and planetary science. The work focuses on using satellite imagery, topographic data, and machine learning to better understand landscapes on Earth and Mars.

By combining computer vision, geospatial analysis, and high-performance computing, these research efforts support automated landform mapping, surface-process interpretation, and scalable analysis of large remote-sensing datasets.

The broader goal is to develop reproducible and interpretable AI workflows that help transform complex spatial imagery into scientific insight for geomorphology, environmental monitoring, and planetary exploration.

Research Focus

GeoAI and remote sensing provide new opportunities to study surface processes at large spatial scales.

Current directions include planetary landform detection, Earth observation, interpretable AI, and reproducible geospatial workflows for scientific discovery.

Research Themes

Exploring landscapes through data, imagery, and machine intelligence.

The research direction connects AI, remote sensing, and geospatial science to analyze surface forms, detect patterns, and interpret environmental and planetary processes from large-scale spatial datasets.

Planetary GeoAI

Deep learning and computer vision methods for mapping Martian landforms, analyzing planetary surface morphology, and supporting large-scale geomorphic interpretation from orbital imagery.

Remote Sensing

Multi-sensor analysis using optical, radar, hyperspectral, and topographic datasets to study surface processes, environmental change, terrain structure, and planetary landscapes.

Interpretable AI

Scalable, reproducible, and interpretable AI workflows for scientific discovery, including instance segmentation, spatial analysis, and data-driven terrain characterization.

From satellite imagery to scientific understanding.

Modern Earth and planetary datasets are growing rapidly in volume and complexity. AI-driven geospatial methods can help identify subtle spatial patterns, map landforms at scale, and support more efficient scientific interpretation of remote-sensing observations.
